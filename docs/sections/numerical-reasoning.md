# Numerical reasoning

**In one line:** you work with numbers, percentages, ratios, series and tables under a tight clock, and pick the right answer from a short list.

TestGorilla lists its Numerical Reasoning test as a 10-minute, intermediate-level test. It is one of the cognitive tests employers add most often for roles that involve spreadsheets, budgets, targets or reports.

## What it measures

TestGorilla says the test covers four skills:

- interpreting numbers, fractions, ratios and percentages
- understanding number series and patterns
- reading tables, charts, graphs and diagrams
- basic arithmetic: adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing

It names four question types: numerical computation, numerical estimation, numerical reasoning and data interpretation. In plain terms, some questions want an exact answer, some want the closest answer, some are short word problems, and some hang off a table or chart.

None of this goes beyond school maths. What makes it hard is doing it quickly and without slips.

## How the questions look

Each question sits on its own screen with a short list of answers. You click one and submit. You cannot go back.

| Type | What you see | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Computation | A sum written out, such as "15% of 360 plus 3/8 of 96" | Work it out exactly |
| Estimation | A sum with awkward numbers, such as 4,987 × 0.198 | Round and pick the closest option |
| Reasoning | A short word problem: rates, ratios, percentage change | Set it up, then calculate |
| Data interpretation | A small table or chart, then a question about it | Find the right cells, then calculate |
| Number series | A row of numbers with one missing | Spot the rule and apply it |

TestGorilla's candidate guide says you **may use pen, paper and a calculator** during the assessment. Have all three ready. A calculator does not save you from setting the problem up wrong, but it does save you from arithmetic slips.

## A method that works

1. **Read the question line before the data.** Know what you are hunting for before you look at a table. "Percentage increase from Q1 to Q4" tells you which two columns matter.
2. **Write the setup, not just the numbers.** One line on paper, such as "West Q3 ÷ South Q3", stops you dividing the wrong way round.
3. **Estimate first.** Before you press any calculator keys, decide roughly what the answer should be. If the calculator disagrees wildly, you typed something wrong.
4. **Use the options.** If the options are far apart, a rough estimate is enough. If two options are close, do the exact sum.
5. **Check the unit and the base.** Percent of what? Minutes or hours? Per item or in total?
6. **Submit and move on.** If you are stuck after about 45 seconds, pick the option closest to your estimate. A skipped question counts as wrong, and TestGorilla says there is no penalty for a wrong answer.

## Traps that cost points

- **Wrong base for a percentage.** "A is what percent of B" means A ÷ B. Reversing it is the most common slip, and test writers usually put the reversed answer among the options.
- **Adding percentage changes.** A 20% rise followed by a 20% fall is not zero. It is a 4% fall, because the fall is taken from a bigger number.
- **Averaging speeds or rates.** Average speed is total distance divided by total time, not the average of two speeds.
- **Percentage increase versus increase.** A rise from 120 to 195 is 75 units, but a 62.5% increase. Read which one the question wants.
- **Reading the wrong row or column.** Tables with similar labels (Q1, Q2, Q3) invite this. Put your finger, or cursor, on the cell.
- **Over-calculating an estimation question.** If the options are 100, 1,000, 1,250 and 10,000, you only need to know the answer is close to 1,000.

## Timing

TestGorilla gives the test 10 minutes but does not publish how many questions it contains, and the timer may run per test or per question. As guidance, aim for about 45 to 60 seconds on a table question and about 30 seconds on a straight calculation. Use the four practice questions at the start of the test to see the layout and the timer, since they do not count toward your score.

## Practice items

These 12 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from TestGorilla or any publisher. Use a calculator if you like, since the real test allows one, but set a timer for 10 minutes.

**Item 1.** What is 15% of 360 plus 3/8 of 96?

- A. 84
- B. 88
- C. 90
- D. 96

**Item 2.** Which is closest to 4,987 × 0.198?

- A. 100
- B. 1,000
- C. 1,250
- D. 10,000

**Item 3.** What number comes next? 3, 7, 15, 31, 63, ?

- A. 95
- B. 126
- C. 128
- D. 127

**Item 4.** A bonus pool is split between three people in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5. The smallest share is 1,400. What is the whole pool?

- A. 7,000
- B. 4,200
- C. 9,800
- D. 14,000

**Item 5.** The price of a part rises by 20%, then falls by 20%. Compared with the original price, what is the overall change?

- A. No change
- B. A 2% fall
- C. A 4% fall
- D. A 4% rise

Items 6 to 9 use this table of units sold.

| Region | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North | 120 | 150 | 135 | 195 |
| South | 200 | 180 | 210 | 210 |
| West | 80 | 100 | 125 | 95 |

**Item 6.** How many units were sold in Q3 across all three regions?

- A. 450
- B. 465
- C. 480
- D. 470

**Item 7.** Which region had the largest percentage increase in units sold from Q1 to Q4?

- A. North
- B. South
- C. West
- D. North and West are equal

**Item 8.** West's Q3 sales were roughly what percentage of South's Q3 sales?

- A. 50%
- B. 60%
- C. 65%
- D. 168%

**Item 9.** What was North's average number of units sold per quarter?

- A. 140
- B. 155
- C. 150
- D. 160

**Item 10.** Printer A prints 40 pages a minute and printer B prints 60 pages a minute. Working together, how long do they take to print 1,500 pages?

- A. 12 minutes
- B. 15 minutes
- C. 18 minutes
- D. 25 minutes

**Item 11.** A van drives 180 km at 60 km/h, then 80 km at 40 km/h. What is its average speed for the whole trip?

- A. 50 km/h
- B. 54 km/h
- C. 52 km/h
- D. 56 km/h

**Item 12.** In a survey of 2,400 staff, 35% chose option X. One quarter of the people who chose option X are managers. How many managers chose option X?

- A. 84
- B. 240
- C. 600
- D. 210

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 12</summary>

**Item 1: C (90).** 15% of 360 is 0.15 × 360 = 54. 3/8 of 96 is 96 ÷ 8 × 3 = 36. 54 + 36 = 90. *Why not B:* 88 comes from rounding 15% of 360 down to 52, a typical mental-maths slip.

**Item 2: B (1,000).** Round to 5,000 × 0.2 = 1,000. The exact answer is 987.426, which is far closer to 1,000 than to 1,250. *Why not C:* 1,250 would need a multiplier of 0.25.

**Item 3: D (127).** Each number is double the one before, plus 1: 3 × 2 + 1 = 7, 7 × 2 + 1 = 15, and so on. 63 × 2 + 1 = 127. *Why not B or C:* 126 and 128 come from doubling only, or doubling and adding 2.

**Item 4: A (7,000).** The smallest share is 2 parts, so one part is 1,400 ÷ 2 = 700. The pool has 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 parts, so 10 × 700 = 7,000. *Why not D:* 14,000 treats 1,400 as one part instead of two.

**Item 5: C (a 4% fall).** Start with 100. A 20% rise gives 120. A 20% fall takes 20% of 120, which is 24, leaving 96. That is 4% below the start. *Why not A:* the two 20% changes are taken from different bases, so they do not cancel.

**Item 6: D (470).** Q3 column: 135 + 210 + 125 = 470. *Why not B:* 465 is a slip from adding 130 instead of 135.

**Item 7: A (North).** North: (195 − 120) ÷ 120 = 75 ÷ 120 = 62.5%. South: 10 ÷ 200 = 5%. West: 15 ÷ 80 = 18.75%. North is far ahead. *Why not C:* West grew a lot during the year but fell back in Q4.

**Item 8: B (60%).** 125 ÷ 210 = 0.595, about 60%. *Why not D:* 168% is 210 ÷ 125, the question turned upside down.

**Item 9: C (150).** 120 + 150 + 135 + 195 = 600. 600 ÷ 4 = 150.

**Item 10: B (15 minutes).** Together they print 40 + 60 = 100 pages a minute. 1,500 ÷ 100 = 15. *Why not D:* 25 minutes is printer A alone at 60 pages a minute, a mix-up of the two rates.

**Item 11: C (52 km/h).** Time for the first leg: 180 ÷ 60 = 3 hours. Second leg: 80 ÷ 40 = 2 hours. Total distance 260 km in 5 hours, so 260 ÷ 5 = 52 km/h. *Why not A:* 50 is the simple average of 60 and 40, which ignores that the van spent more time at 60 km/h.

**Item 12: D (210).** 35% of 2,400 is 840. One quarter of 840 is 210. *Why not C:* 600 is one quarter of all 2,400 staff, not of the 840 who chose X.

</details>
