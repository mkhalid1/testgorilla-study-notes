# Problem solving

**In one line:** you solve small workplace puzzles: building a schedule, ranking tasks by rules, and deciding something from a short table or message.

TestGorilla lists its Problem Solving test as a 9-minute, intermediate-level, multiple-choice test, the shortest of its cognitive tests. It is aimed at roles with changing variables and tight deadlines. TestGorilla's examples include administrative assistants, project managers, customer service managers, web developers, hospitality and sales staff.

## What it measures

TestGorilla says the test covers:

- building and revising schedules
- reading data and using logic to make decisions
- ranking tasks according to a set of rules
- reviewing text and numbers to reach conclusions

Its scenarios cover scheduling, ordering actions under business rules, and drawing conclusions from text and numerical information. In other words: everyday office logic, under time pressure.

## How the questions look

| Type | What you get | What you decide |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduling | Slots, tasks and a few constraints | Which task goes where, or what time something happens |
| Rule-based ranking | A list of items and the rules for ordering them | Which item comes first, second or last |
| Data to decision | A small timetable, table or capacity limit | The option that meets the goal |
| Revising a plan | A plan, then a change | What the change does to the result |
| Text and numbers | A short message with figures | What is needed, or whether a target is met |

## A method that works

1. **Find the goal in the question line.** "Latest train that still gets you there by 9:00" is a different job from "fastest train".
2. **Write the constraints as a numbered list.** Short forms are fine: "1. Legal not 11. 2. Finance before Legal. 3. HR at 10 or 11."
3. **Apply the strictest constraint first.** A rule that fixes something outright, such as "S is first" or "Z is on Wednesday", removes the most options.
4. **For ranking, apply rules in the order given.** A tie-break rule only matters after the main rule has been applied.
5. **For timings, add the whole chain.** Departure plus journey plus walk. Most wrong answers leave one step out.
6. **Check the final answer against every rule once.** It takes five seconds and catches most mistakes.

## Traps that cost points

- **Forgetting a step in a time chain.** The train arrives at 8:57, but the walk takes 10 minutes.
- **Using a tie-break as the main rule.** If the rule ranks by experience first and test score second, a high score does not beat more experience.
- **Missing a pass-or-fail rule.** "Must have a licence" removes a candidate entirely, however strong they are otherwise.
- **Treating tasks that can run at the same time as one after another.** If task C can run alongside A and B, the project is not A + B + C long.
- **Answering what is likely instead of what the rules say.** These are logic puzzles. The rules decide, not your view of what a sensible manager would do.

## Timing

Nine minutes is short, and TestGorilla does not publish the number of questions. As guidance, aim for about 45 seconds per question, and up to 75 seconds for a scheduling puzzle with several constraints. If a puzzle has not come together after that, test the options against the rules one by one. Often only one option survives.

## Practice items

These 3 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from TestGorilla or any publisher.

**Item 1.** Support tickets are handled by these rules. Rule 1: outages come before everything else. Rule 2: then paying customers come before free users. Rule 3: within the same group, the oldest ticket goes first. Today's tickets:

| Ticket | Customer | Issue | Opened |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Free | Login | 9:05 |
| T2 | Paying | Billing | 9:20 |
| T3 | Free | Outage | 9:30 |
| T4 | Paying | Login | 9:10 |

Which ticket is handled second?

- A. T1
- B. T2
- C. T3
- D. T4

**Item 2.** Trains leave Hillside at 8:05, 8:20, 8:35 and 8:50. The journey takes 22 minutes, and the walk from the station to the office takes 10 minutes. You must be at the office by 9:00. What is the latest train you can take?

- A. 8:05
- B. 8:35
- C. 8:20
- D. 8:50

**Item 3.** Five deliveries, V, W, X, Y and Z, go out one per day, Monday to Friday. Z goes on Wednesday. X does not go on Monday. Y goes the day after X. W goes before X. On which day does Y go?

- A. Tuesday
- B. Thursday
- C. Wednesday
- D. Friday

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 3</summary>

**Item 1: D (T4).** Rule 1 puts the outage, T3, first. Rule 2 puts paying customers next: T2 and T4. Rule 3 orders them by age: T4 opened at 9:10, before T2 at 9:20. So the order is T3, T4, T2, T1. *Why not C:* T3 is first, not second.

**Item 2: C (8:20).** The 8:20 arrives at 8:42, and the walk puts you at the office at 8:52. The 8:35 arrives at 8:57, and the walk makes it 9:07, which is late. *Why not B:* it forgets the walk.

**Item 3: D (Friday).** X cannot be Monday, and it cannot be Wednesday, because that is Z's day. If X were Tuesday, Y would be Wednesday, which is also taken. If X were Friday, Y would have no day left. So X is Thursday and Y is Friday. W and V fill Monday and Tuesday, but the question only asks about Y.

</details>
