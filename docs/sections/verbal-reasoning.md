# Verbal reasoning

**In one line:** you spot how words relate to each other, then decide which conclusions really follow from a short piece of text.

TestGorilla lists its Verbal Reasoning test as a 10-minute, intermediate-level, multiple-choice test. It is used for roles where reading and clear language matter, and TestGorilla's own examples range from account managers and analysts to nurses, support agents and drivers.

## What it measures

TestGorilla says the test covers:

- solving problems using words and language
- finding analogies
- applying logical reasoning
- understanding premises and inference

It also says the test has **two parts**. The first is about identifying and replicating relationships between words. The second is about drawing valid conclusions from text you read.

So you need two habits: naming the exact link between two words, and refusing to accept a conclusion that the text does not actually support.

## How the questions look

**Part 1, word relationships.** You see a pair of words and must find the word or pair that has the same relationship. Typical forms:

| Form | Example shape | What to find |
|---|---|---|
| Analogy | A is to B as C is to ? | The word that links to C the same way B links to A |
| Pair match | Which pair is like A : B? | The pair with the same relationship |
| Synonym or opposite | Closest in meaning to, or opposite of | One word that matches the meaning asked for |
| Odd one out | Which word does not belong? | The one word that breaks the shared meaning |

**Part 2, conclusions from text.** You read a few sentences of facts, then pick the conclusion that must be true, or the one that cannot be worked out. Treat the text as true, even if it sounds odd, and use nothing beyond it.

## A method that works

**For word relationships:**

1. **Say the relationship as a sentence.** "A thermometer measures temperature." Vague links like "they are related" lead to wrong answers.
2. **Apply the same sentence to each option.** "An odometer measures ___." Only one option fits the sentence exactly.
3. **If two options fit, make the sentence stricter.** "A chapter is one part of a book" beats "a chapter is in a book".
4. **Check direction.** Part to whole is not the same as whole to part. Maker to product is not product to maker.

**For conclusions:**

1. **List the facts in short form.** For example: "safety course → badge; no badge → no entry; Priya entered."
2. **Ask "must this be true?"** for each option, not "is this likely?". If you can picture the facts staying true while the option is false, it does not follow.
3. **Watch "some" and "all".** "Some" means at least one, possibly all. "All A are B" does not mean all B are A.
4. **For "cannot be determined" questions,** look for the option the text never settles either way.

## Traps that cost points

- **Using real-world knowledge.** If the text says something you know is false, you still accept it for the question. If the text is silent, your own knowledge does not fill the gap.
- **Reversing "all".** "Everyone who completes the course gets a badge" does not mean everyone with a badge completed the course.
- **Likely versus must.** A conclusion that is probably true is still wrong when the question asks what must be true.
- **Picking a related word instead of the matching relationship.** Nest, shell and yolk are all linked to an egg, but only "bird" matches "seed is to tree".
- **Missing "not" or "except".** "Which cannot be determined" and "which must be true" flip the job. Reread the question line before you answer.

## Timing

TestGorilla does not publish the number of questions in this test. As guidance, word-relationship items should take 15 to 25 seconds each once you are practised, which buys time for the text items, where 45 to 60 seconds is reasonable. If an analogy has not clicked after 30 seconds, pick the option that best fits your relationship sentence and move on.

## Practice items

These 3 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from TestGorilla or any publisher. Items 1 and 2 are word relationships. Item 3 is a conclusion from text.

**Item 1.** SCARCE is to ABUNDANT as TIMID is to:

- A. shy
- B. quiet
- C. bold
- D. afraid

**Item 2.** Which pair has the same relationship as BAKER : BREAD?

- A. doctor : hospital
- B. author : novel
- C. teacher : school
- D. farmer : tractor

For item 3, read the text and treat it as true.

> Every employee who completes the safety course receives a site badge. No one can enter the warehouse without a site badge. Priya entered the warehouse on Monday.

**Item 3.** Which statement must be true?

- A. Priya completed the safety course.
- B. Priya works in the warehouse.
- C. Priya has a site badge.
- D. Priya is a manager.

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 3</summary>

**Item 1: C (bold).** Scarce and abundant are opposites. The opposite of timid is bold. *Why not A or D:* shy and afraid are close in meaning to timid, the reverse of the relationship asked for.

**Item 2: B (author : novel).** A baker makes bread. An author makes a novel. The relationship is maker to product. *Why not A or C:* a doctor works in a hospital and a teacher in a school, which is person to workplace. *Why not D:* a farmer uses a tractor, which is person to tool.

**Item 3: C.** No one can enter without a badge, and Priya entered, so she has a badge. *Why not A:* the text says the course leads to a badge, not that the course is the only way to get one. Visitors or contractors could have badges too. This is the "reversing all" trap.

</details>
