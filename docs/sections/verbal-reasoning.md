# Verbal reasoning

**In one line:** you spot how words relate to each other, then decide which conclusions really follow from a short piece of text.

TestGorilla lists its Verbal Reasoning test as a 10-minute, intermediate-level, multiple-choice test. It is used for roles where reading and clear language matter, and TestGorilla's own examples range from account managers and analysts to nurses, support agents and drivers.

## What it measures

TestGorilla says the test covers:

- solving problems using words and language
- finding analogies
- applying logical reasoning
- understanding premises and inference

It also says the test has **two parts**. The first is about identifying and replicating relationships between words. The second is about drawing valid conclusions from text you read.

So you need two habits: naming the exact link between two words, and refusing to accept a conclusion that the text does not actually support.

## How the questions look

**Part 1, word relationships.** You see a pair of words and must find the word or pair that has the same relationship. Typical forms:

| Form | Example shape | What to find |
|---|---|---|
| Analogy | A is to B as C is to ? | The word that links to C the same way B links to A |
| Pair match | Which pair is like A : B? | The pair with the same relationship |
| Synonym or opposite | Closest in meaning to, or opposite of | One word that matches the meaning asked for |
| Odd one out | Which word does not belong? | The one word that breaks the shared meaning |

**Part 2, conclusions from text.** You read a few sentences of facts, then pick the conclusion that must be true, or the one that cannot be worked out. Treat the text as true, even if it sounds odd, and use nothing beyond it.

## A method that works

**For word relationships:**

1. **Say the relationship as a sentence.** "A thermometer measures temperature." Vague links like "they are related" lead to wrong answers.
2. **Apply the same sentence to each option.** "An odometer measures ___." Only one option fits the sentence exactly.
3. **If two options fit, make the sentence stricter.** "A chapter is one part of a book" beats "a chapter is in a book".
4. **Check direction.** Part to whole is not the same as whole to part. Maker to product is not product to maker.

**For conclusions:**

1. **List the facts in short form.** For example: "safety course → badge; no badge → no entry; Priya entered."
2. **Ask "must this be true?"** for each option, not "is this likely?". If you can picture the facts staying true while the option is false, it does not follow.
3. **Watch "some" and "all".** "Some" means at least one, possibly all. "All A are B" does not mean all B are A.
4. **For "cannot be determined" questions,** look for the option the text never settles either way.

## Traps that cost points

- **Using real-world knowledge.** If the text says something you know is false, you still accept it for the question. If the text is silent, your own knowledge does not fill the gap.
- **Reversing "all".** "Everyone who completes the course gets a badge" does not mean everyone with a badge completed the course.
- **Likely versus must.** A conclusion that is probably true is still wrong when the question asks what must be true.
- **Picking a related word instead of the matching relationship.** Nest, shell and yolk are all linked to an egg, but only "bird" matches "seed is to tree".
- **Missing "not" or "except".** "Which cannot be determined" and "which must be true" flip the job. Reread the question line before you answer.

## Timing

TestGorilla does not publish the number of questions in this test. As guidance, word-relationship items should take 15 to 25 seconds each once you are practised, which buys time for the text items, where 45 to 60 seconds is reasonable. If an analogy has not clicked after 30 seconds, pick the option that best fits your relationship sentence and move on.

## Practice items

These 12 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from TestGorilla or any publisher. Items 1 to 6 are word relationships. Items 7 to 12 are conclusions from text.

**Item 1.** THERMOMETER is to TEMPERATURE as ODOMETER is to:

- A. speed
- B. distance
- C. fuel
- D. time

**Item 2.** SCARCE is to ABUNDANT as TIMID is to:

- A. shy
- B. quiet
- C. bold
- D. afraid

**Item 3.** CHAPTER is to BOOK as VERSE is to:

- A. poem
- B. rhyme
- C. poet
- D. line

**Item 4.** Which pair has the same relationship as BAKER : BREAD?

- A. doctor : hospital
- B. author : novel
- C. teacher : school
- D. farmer : tractor

**Item 5.** ERODE is to WEAR AWAY as AMPLIFY is to:

- A. reduce
- B. repeat
- C. explain
- D. enlarge

**Item 6.** Which word does not belong with the others?

- A. lethargic
- B. vigilant
- C. sluggish
- D. listless

For item 7, read the text and treat it as true.

> Every employee who completes the safety course receives a site badge. No one can enter the warehouse without a site badge. Priya entered the warehouse on Monday.

**Item 7.** Which statement must be true?

- A. Priya completed the safety course.
- B. Priya works in the warehouse.
- C. Priya has a site badge.
- D. Priya is a manager.

For items 8 and 9, read the text and treat it as true.

> The library opens at 9 a.m. on weekdays and at 10 a.m. on Saturdays. It is closed on Sundays. Last Tuesday it opened two hours late because of a power cut.

**Item 8.** Which statement follows from the text?

- A. The library opened at 11 a.m. last Tuesday.
- B. The library opens later on Saturdays than on weekdays.
- C. Power cuts often affect the library.
- D. Both A and B.

**Item 9.** Which of these cannot be worked out from the text?

- A. Whether the library was open last Sunday
- B. What time the library opened last Tuesday
- C. Whether the library opened late on any other day last week
- D. What time the library opens on Saturdays

For items 10 and 11, read the text and treat it as true.

> Some of the trainees speak Spanish. All the trainees who speak Spanish were assigned to the Madrid project. No one on the Madrid project works night shifts.

**Item 10.** Which statement must be true?

- A. No trainee works night shifts.
- B. All trainees are on the Madrid project.
- C. Some people on the Madrid project do not speak Spanish.
- D. Some trainees do not work night shifts.

**Item 11.** Marco is a trainee who works night shifts. What follows?

- A. Marco speaks Spanish.
- B. Marco does not speak Spanish.
- C. Marco is on the Madrid project.
- D. Nothing can be concluded about Marco.

For item 12, read the text and treat it as true.

> Every drink the café sells is made with either oat milk or dairy milk. Since the café began offering oat milk at no extra charge, sales of oat-milk drinks have tripled, while sales of dairy-milk drinks have stayed the same.

**Item 12.** Which conclusion is best supported by the text?

- A. Customers now prefer oat milk to dairy milk.
- B. The café's total drink sales have risen.
- C. The café will soon stop selling dairy milk.
- D. Oat milk is healthier than dairy milk.

<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 12</summary>

**Item 1: B (distance).** A thermometer measures temperature. An odometer measures distance travelled. *Why not A:* speed is measured by a speedometer, a close neighbour that the item is built to catch.

**Item 2: C (bold).** Scarce and abundant are opposites. The opposite of timid is bold. *Why not A or D:* shy and afraid are close in meaning to timid, the reverse of the relationship asked for.

**Item 3: A (poem).** A chapter is one part of a book. A verse is one part of a poem. *Why not D:* a verse is made of lines, so "line" reverses the relationship: a line is part of a verse, not the whole.

**Item 4: B (author : novel).** A baker makes bread. An author makes a novel. The relationship is maker to product. *Why not A or C:* a doctor works in a hospital and a teacher in a school, which is person to workplace. *Why not D:* a farmer uses a tractor, which is person to tool.

**Item 5: D (enlarge).** To erode is to wear away: the second item means the same as the first. To amplify is to enlarge or make stronger. *Why not A:* reduce is the opposite.

**Item 6: B (vigilant).** Lethargic, sluggish and listless all describe a lack of energy. Vigilant means watchful and alert, the odd one out.

**Item 7: C.** No one can enter without a badge, and Priya entered, so she has a badge. *Why not A:* the text says the course leads to a badge, not that the course is the only way to get one. Visitors or contractors could have badges too. This is the "reversing all" trap.

**Item 8: D.** A: last Tuesday was a weekday, so the normal time is 9 a.m., and two hours late is 11 a.m. B: 10 a.m. on Saturdays is later than 9 a.m. on weekdays. Both follow. *Why not C:* one power cut says nothing about how often they happen.

**Item 9: C.** The text mentions only last Tuesday's late opening. It does not say whether the library opened late on any other day. *Why not A:* it is closed every Sunday, so we know it was closed. B and D are stated or follow directly.

**Item 10: D.** At least one trainee speaks Spanish. Every Spanish-speaking trainee is on the Madrid project, and no one on that project works nights. So at least one trainee does not work night shifts. *Why not A:* trainees who do not speak Spanish could be on any project and could work nights. *Why not C:* nothing says anyone on the project lacks Spanish.

**Item 11: B.** If Marco spoke Spanish, he would be on the Madrid project, and then he could not work night shifts. He does work night shifts, so he does not speak Spanish. *Why not D:* the chain of rules does let us rule Spanish out.

**Item 12: B.** Every drink is either oat or dairy. Oat-milk drinks tripled, which means they rose from some number above zero, and dairy-milk drinks stayed the same. So total drink sales rose. *Why not A:* dairy sales held steady, and the change could be driven by the free oat milk, not a general preference. *Why not C and D:* the text says nothing about plans or health.

</details>

- **11 to 12:** strong. Keep your speed up on the word items so you have time for the text.
- **8 to 10:** if you missed word items, practise saying the relationship as a full sentence. If you missed text items, ask "must this be true?" every time, never "is this likely?".
- **7 or fewer:** start with items 7, 10 and 11. Draw the rules as arrows on paper and follow them. Most misses here come from reversing an "all" statement.
