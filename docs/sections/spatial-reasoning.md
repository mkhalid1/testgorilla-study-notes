# Spatial reasoning

**In one line:** you turn, fold and track shapes in your head, then pick what they would look like or where they would end up.

TestGorilla lists its Spatial Reasoning test as a 10-minute, intermediate-level test, suggested for engineering, research, design, architecture and other STEM roles. Its page does not describe the question format, and the real test uses images. The practice items here are drawn as text grids, so you can train the same mental moves without pictures.

## What it measures

TestGorilla says the test covers four skills:

- **mental rotation:** picturing a shape after it has been turned
- **spatial working memory:** keeping track of positions and directions as they change
- **mental folding:** picturing what a flat shape looks like when folded, or a folded shape when opened
- **spatial visualisation:** building a 3D picture from a description or a flat plan

## How the questions look

Expect a shape or a short description, then several options to choose from. In picture form, the common tasks are:

| Task | You see | You decide |
|---|---|---|
| Rotation | A shape and four turned versions | Which one is the same shape, turned, not flipped |
| Folding and punching | A sheet folded, then punched | Where the holes are when it is opened |
| Cube nets | A flat net of six faces | Which faces end up opposite, or which cube it makes |
| Counting cubes | A built-up block of cubes | How many cubes, or how many with painted faces |
| Directions and paths | A series of turns or moves | Which way you face, or where you end up |

## A method that works

1. **Track one feature, not the whole shape.** Pick a corner or a distinctive square, follow where it goes, then check the rest.
2. **For rotations, turn in steps of 90 degrees.** A 180-degree turn is two quarter turns. A 270-degree clockwise turn is one quarter turn anticlockwise.
3. **Tell rotation from reflection.** A turned shape keeps its handedness. If an option only matches as a mirror image, it is wrong in a rotation question.
4. **For folds, work backwards.** Each fold doubles the layers. Every hole punched through the folded paper becomes one hole per layer, mirrored across each fold line.
5. **For cube nets, find the faces in a straight line.** In a row of three faces, the first and third end up opposite each other.
6. **Use paper.** TestGorilla's candidate guide allows pen and paper. A quick sketch of a grid or a compass rose is often faster than holding it all in your head.

## Traps that cost points

- **Choosing the mirror image.** It looks almost right, which is exactly why it is offered.
- **Turning the wrong way.** Decide clockwise or anticlockwise before you start, and say it to yourself.
- **Forgetting the hidden layer in a fold.** A punch through two layers makes two holes, even though you only see one on top.
- **Counting faces next to each other as opposite.** On a net, faces that touch along an edge are neighbours on the cube, never opposite.
- **Losing count on painted-cube questions.** Use the rule: corners have 3 painted faces, edges have 2, face centres have 1, and the core has none.

## Timing

TestGorilla does not publish the number of questions. As guidance, aim for about 30 to 45 seconds per item. Spatial items can eat time if you try to see the whole picture at once. If you are stuck, track one corner and eliminate the options it rules out.

## Practice items

These 3 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from TestGorilla or any publisher. In the grids, `X` is a filled square and `.` is empty. Rows are read top to bottom.

**Item 1.** This grid is turned 90 degrees clockwise. What does it look like?

```
X . .
X X .
. . .
```

```
A        B        C        D
. X X    . . .    X X .    . . .
. X .    . X .    . X .    . X X
. . .    X X .    . . .    . . X
```

**Item 2.** A square sheet is folded in half top to bottom, then folded in half again left to right, making a smaller square of four layers. One hole is punched through the centre of the folded square. The sheet is then opened. What do you see?

- A. One hole in the centre of the sheet
- B. Two holes, one in the centre of each half
- C. Four holes, one in the centre of each quarter
- D. Four holes, all along the middle fold line

**Item 3.** A clock with no numbers shows 3:00. You see it in a mirror. What time does the reflection appear to show?

- A. 9:00
- B. 3:00
- C. 6:00
- D. 12:15

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 3</summary>

**Item 1: A.** Track the left column, which reads X, X, empty from top to bottom. A clockwise turn moves the left column to the top row, read from right to left: so the top row becomes empty, X, X. The centre square stays in the centre. That gives A. *Why not C:* C is the mirror image of A, flipped left to right.

**Item 2: C.** Two folds make four layers, one for each quarter of the sheet. A hole through the centre of the folded square goes through all four layers, landing in the centre of each quarter. Unfolded, the holes mirror each other across both fold lines.

**Item 3: A (9:00).** A mirror flips left and right. At 3:00 the hour hand points right, so in the mirror it points left, which is where it would be at 9:00. The minute hand points straight up in both. A quick rule: for an exact hour, the mirror time is 12 minus the real time.

</details>

- **2:** find the move that tripped you: rotation, folding or nets. Do five more of that kind with a sketch beside you.
- **0 or 1:** build the habit of tracking one corner. Draw each step on paper until you can see it without drawing.
