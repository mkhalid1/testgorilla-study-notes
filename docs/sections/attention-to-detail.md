# Attention to detail

**In one line:** you compare names, numbers, codes and short texts, and catch the small differences, errors and inconsistencies that most people read straight past.

TestGorilla has two attention to detail tests: **Textual** and **Visual**. This page covers the textual one, which is the one most often added for office, finance and admin roles. TestGorilla lists it at intermediate level. Its page shows 12 minutes in the header and calls it "this 10-minute assessment test" in the body, so check the time shown on your own welcome screen. The visual version is listed as 10 minutes.

## What it measures

TestGorilla says the textual test covers:

- matching information
- comparing statements for differences
- checking the consistency of information

Candidates are asked to find errors, omissions or inconsistencies in the information shown. TestGorilla suggests the test for roles such as accountants, editors, quality assurance staff, software engineers, data analysts, customer care staff and administrators, and says it suits entry-level jobs too.

## How the questions look

TestGorilla does not publish a question format for this test, so here are the kinds of task the skills list points to, shown in our own words:

| Task | What you see | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Exact match | An original and several near copies | Pick the copy that matches exactly |
| Pair check | A list of pairs of names, numbers or emails | Count how many pairs are identical |
| Rule check | A rule, such as a code format, and some examples | Find the one that breaks the rule |
| Cross-document check | Two versions of the same order, email or record | Find the detail that differs |
| Consistency check | A schedule, list or statement | Find the date, total or order that does not fit |

## A method that works

1. **Compare in chunks, not as a whole.** Split long strings into pieces of three or four characters: "INV-2093" then "1-KX". Your eye skims whole words but reads small chunks properly.
2. **Check numbers digit by digit.** Swapped digits (117 and 171, 2QT and 2TQ) are the most common planted error.
3. **Read names letter by letter.** Look at the vowels: Olsen and Olson, Northfield and Northfeild.
4. **For rules, turn the rule into a checklist.** "Two capital letters, a hyphen, four digits" becomes three checks. Run each example through all three.
5. **For two documents, go field by field.** Order number, then item, then quantity, then date. Do not reread the whole text each time.
6. **Use paper for counts.** When asked how many pairs match, mark a tick or a cross for each pair as you go, then count the ticks.

## Traps that cost points

- **Reading what you expect.** Your brain fixes typos for you. Slow down on familiar words, because those are where errors hide.
- **Stopping at the first difference.** In "which detail is different" questions, there can be one planted difference among several near-identical fields. Find it, but in "how many match" questions keep checking every pair.
- **Ignoring capitals, punctuation and spaces.** "Pd-5512" is not the same format as "PD-5512". A missing hyphen is a real difference.
- **Confusing similar characters.** 0 and O, 1 and l, 5 and S, rn and m.
- **Skipping the question line.** "Which matches" and "which does not match" need opposite answers.

## Timing

TestGorilla does not publish the number of questions. As guidance, a single match question should take 15 to 20 seconds, and a pair-count or two-document question 30 to 45 seconds. Speed comes from a fixed routine, not from reading faster. If you rush, you start to fix errors in your head instead of seeing them.

## Practice items

These 3 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from TestGorilla or any publisher.

**Item 1.** Which option matches this address exactly?

`Flat 4B, 117 Harrow Lane, Leeds LS6 2QT`

- A. `Flat 4B, 117 Harrow Lane, Leeds LS6 2TQ`
- B. `Flat 4B, 117 Harrow Lane, Leeds LS6 2QT`
- C. `Flat 4B, 171 Harrow Lane, Leeds LS6 2QT`
- D. `Flat 4B, 117 Harrow Lane, Leeds LS2 6QT`

**Item 2.** Product codes must be two capital letters, a hyphen, then four digits. Which code breaks the rule?

- A. KT-4821
- B. MR-0937
- C. Pd-5512
- D. ZX-7604

**Item 3.** A project plan for 2026 lists these dates. Which entry is inconsistent?

```
Kickoff:  Monday 2 March 2026
Review:   Monday 9 March 2026
Launch:   Monday 17 March 2026
```

- A. Kickoff
- B. Review
- C. Launch
- D. None, all three are consistent

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 3</summary>

**Item 1: B.** A swaps the last two letters of the postcode (2TQ). C swaps the house number digits (171). D swaps the postcode districts (LS2 6QT). Only B is identical.

**Item 2: C.** "Pd" has a lowercase d, so it is not two capital letters. The others all have two capitals, a hyphen and four digits.

**Item 3: C.** 2 March 2026 is a Monday, so 9 March is too, and 16 March is the next Monday. 17 March 2026 is a Tuesday, so "Monday 17 March" cannot be right. Count on in sevens from a date you trust: 2, 9, 16, 23.

</details>
