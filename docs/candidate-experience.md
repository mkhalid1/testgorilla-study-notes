# The candidate experience, step by step

Most TestGorilla candidates have never used the platform before, and a lot of nerves come from not knowing what the screens will ask. This page walks through an assessment from the invitation email to the results, using TestGorilla's own candidate guides. Knowing it in advance means none of it eats into your concentration on the day.

## The invitation and reminders

- The invitation comes from TestGorilla with the subject line **"Your application at"** followed by the company name.
- It names the role and the company, lists what the assessment contains, and shows how long you will have.
- You do **not** need an account. You click the link in the email.
- If you have not started, TestGorilla sends a reminder within 48 hours, with the subject **"Important: your assessment is waiting"**. TestGorilla says it never sends more than two reminders per assessment.
- The employer may set a deadline. If you cannot meet it, contact the employer, not TestGorilla.

## Setting up

Before the first test you pass through a few setup screens. Not every assessment shows all of them.

1. **Landing page.** Enter your name and email and accept the privacy policy. The employer may have added a welcome video.
2. **Welcome screen.** Shows how many timed tests and questions you have, and the expected total time. TestGorilla says it may take you less time than that, but not more.
3. **Accommodations.** If the employer has turned this on, you are asked whether you are fluent in the language of the assessment, and whether you have a condition that affects memory or concentration. TestGorilla says the employer is told if you answer that you are not fluent. For the memory or concentration question, you choose whether the employer is told, and if so they learn only which accommodation was given, not the details.
4. **Honesty agreement.** You agree not to use AI tools, not to retake the assessment, and not to break TestGorilla's candidate honesty policy.
5. **Camera and microphone.** TestGorilla uses your camera for periodic snapshots and for video questions. Record a short test video here, as TestGorilla suggests.
6. **Display check.** If the employer requires a single screen, you cannot start until any extra display is unplugged, powered off or set to mirror mode. TestGorilla says this includes anything connected, such as a TV on HDMI or a screen on a docking station.

## During the assessment

- **Full-screen is required.** If you leave full-screen, the current question is hidden until you return, the timer keeps running, and the employer can see the activity.
- **Screenshots and window switching hide the question.** Pressing a screenshot shortcut, switching window or tab, or moving the mouse outside the assessment page hides the question until you click **Show question** again.
- **Practice questions first.** Most tests begin with four practice questions. They do not count, and the employer does not see your answers.
- **One question per screen.** You answer, submit, and move on. You cannot go back.
- **Skipping is possible but costly.** Pressing Next with no answer skips the question after a confirmation message. A skipped question counts as wrong.
- **Pen, paper and a calculator are allowed.** AI and other tools are not.
- **Question types vary.** TestGorilla lists multiple choice, essay, file upload, video, AI interviews, typing and coding as its most common types.

## Between tests

After most tests, TestGorilla asks you to rate the test. This feedback is anonymous and not shown to the employer, and it is not asked after personality tests. Then you choose to take a break or continue. If you take a break, you can close the window and come back through the link in your invitation. Breaks are only possible between tests, not during one.

## At the end

There is a short three-step finish: optional background information (which TestGorilla says the hiring team never sees), an optional rating of the experience, and an invitation to create a TestGorilla profile. You also get an email with the subject **"Your assessment was submitted, what's next?"**.

Your results go to the employer. You can see them on your TestGorilla profile only if the employer has chosen to share them. For next steps in your application, contact the employer: TestGorilla says it has no access to that information.

## What the employer can see about your behaviour

TestGorilla groups each candidate's anti-cheating signals into one of three tiers: **No Issues**, **Some Issues** or **Major Issues**. Signals include full-screen exits, tab switches, second-screen reconnections, a disabled webcam and opened developer tools. Webcam snapshots can be checked automatically for things like another person in view or a blocked camera.

TestGorilla tells employers that none of this is proof of cheating, that a candidate might leave full-screen just to close a notification, and that they should not reject anyone on these signals alone. Still, the easiest way to a clean log is simple:

- turn off notifications and close other apps before you start
- sit somewhere well lit, alone, with your face in view of the camera
- unplug any second screen
- keep your phone out of reach unless you use it as your calculator, and even then a plain calculator is safer

## Practise the flow, not just the questions

The questions are only half the experience. The other half is working fast under a visible timer with no going back. A timed run like the [free TestGorilla-style practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/testgorilla?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=testgorilla) helps you get used to that rhythm before the real assessment.
