# Timing and pacing

A TestGorilla assessment is a run of short, separate timed tests. Each test has its own clock, and some questions may have their own timer too. You cannot go back, and the clock does not stop if you leave the window. Pacing is less about the whole assessment and more about getting each 9 to 13 minute sprint right.

## The budget

These are the listed times for the six tests in this guide. TestGorilla does not publish the number of questions per test, so the per-question figures are our guidance, based on the question types.

| Test | Listed time | Guide per question |
|---|---|---|
| Numerical reasoning | 10 min | 30 s for a sum, 45 to 60 s for a table |
| Verbal reasoning | 10 min | 15 to 25 s for word items, 45 to 60 s for text items |
| Critical thinking | 12 min | 30 to 40 s for syllogisms, 60 to 90 s for arrangements |
| Attention to detail (textual) | 10 or 12 min (the page shows both) | 15 to 20 s for a match, 30 to 45 s for a comparison |
| Spatial reasoning | 10 min | 30 to 45 s |
| Problem solving | 9 min | About 45 s, up to 75 s for a scheduling puzzle |

Your welcome screen shows the expected time for the whole assessment, and each test tells you its time before it starts.

## How the clock works

From TestGorilla's candidate help pages:

- **The timer starts at the beginning of each test** and shows at the top of the screen.
- **A timer may run per test, per question, or both.**
- **If the test timer runs out,** the answers you have already submitted are saved and the assessment moves on. The question you were still working on is not saved, because answers only count once you click submit.
- **The timer keeps running if you leave,** even if you close the window. It cannot be turned off.
- **Breaks are only between tests,** when the platform offers one. Once a test starts, you must finish it.

## A pacing plan for a 10-minute test

| Clock shows | Where you want to be |
|---|---|
| 10:00 | Read the first question. Do not spend long on the instructions; the practice questions already showed you the format. |
| 7:30 | About a quarter of the way through, if you can tell from the question counter. |
| 5:00 | About halfway. If you are well behind, start choosing your best estimate sooner. |
| 1:00 | Finish the question in front of you and submit it. An unsubmitted answer does not count. |

If your screen shows a question counter, use it. If it does not, just keep a steady rhythm and avoid any single question taking more than about a minute and a half.

## Where time usually goes

- **One stubborn question.** Because you cannot go back, it is tempting to fight every question to the end. Set a personal limit, then submit your best guess.
- **Reading instructions twice.** The four practice questions at the start of most tests are there to teach you the format. Use them, then trust them.
- **Calculating what you could estimate.** On numerical questions with spread-out options, an estimate is enough.
- **Recovering from a full-screen exit.** Leaving full-screen hides the question while the timer keeps going. Close notifications and other apps before you start.

## Practise against the clock

Untimed practice teaches the method. Timed practice teaches the pace. Do the practice items in each section guide with a timer, then take a full timed run such as the [free TestGorilla-style practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/testgorilla?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=t7cs68&utm_content=testgorilla), which has 24 questions across four cognitive areas in 18 minutes.
