# How TestGorilla scoring works

Short version: each test gives a raw score (the share of questions you got right) and a percentile (how you did compared with other people who took the same test). Employers can weight some tests more than others, and they decide what score they need. There is no pass mark set by TestGorilla.

## Raw score: percent correct

For a multiple-choice skills test, the raw score is simple: the percentage of questions you answered correctly. TestGorilla's help centre calls this the "Raw score", previously "Percentage", and also "% correct".

Two official rules follow from this:

- **Skipped questions count as wrong.** TestGorilla's candidate guide says so directly.
- **Wrong answers carry no extra penalty.** TestGorilla's candidate FAQ says there isn't a penalty for wrong answers, and suggests guessing rather than skipping.

So an answer, even a guess, is always better than a blank.

## Percentile: compared with others

TestGorilla's default way of showing results to employers is the **percentile**. TestGorilla explains it like this: a candidate in the 54th percentile did better on the test than 54% of the other candidates in its reference group. That is different from a raw score of 54%, which only means 54% of the answers were right.

Why it uses percentiles: each candidate gets a different mix of questions from the question bank, and some mixes are harder than others. TestGorilla says its percentile scores take the difficulty of the questions into account, so a percentile means the same thing whichever questions you were given. Its technology page describes percentiles as reported against calibrated global norms.

One consequence surprises employers too. TestGorilla's help centre answers the question "Why are my candidate scores so low?": with the default "All Candidates" benchmark, a displayed 40% means the candidate beat 40% of everyone who took that test, which may be thousands of people. A percentile of 40 is not a "40% mark".

## What the employer sees

| Score type | What it means | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| Raw score (% correct) | Share of questions answered correctly | Results page and PDF report, and as an option on the job overview |
| Percentile | Share of the reference group you beat, adjusted for question difficulty | Default on the job overview, results page and PDF report |
| Weighted assessment score | A combined score across tests, using weights the employer sets | The candidate list and included-tests section |
| Behaviour tier | No Issues, Some Issues or Major Issues, from the anti-cheating signals | The candidate's behaviour log |

TestGorilla says the results page shows the percentile and raw score side by side, with a bell curve comparing the candidate with others who took the same assessment.

## Weighted scores

Employers can make some tests count more than others. TestGorilla's help centre describes weights from 0 to 5, with every test at an equal weight by default. A weight of 0 keeps a test visible but leaves it out of the overall score. The overall score is a weighted average of the test scores.

What this means for you: the job description is a clue. If the role is a data analyst, the numerical test may well carry the most weight. Put your practice time where the job is.

## Extra time and benchmarks

Employers can give extra time on library tests, up to 90% more. TestGorilla notes that when they do, the scoring benchmark cannot be used, because those candidates are not comparable with people who had the standard time, so the employer compares candidates on % correct only.

If you have a condition that affects memory or concentration, or you are not fluent in the language of the assessment, TestGorilla's setup screen may let you request an accommodation. Not every employer turns this option on.

## What counts as a good score

TestGorilla does not publish a pass mark, and each employer chooses its own threshold, test weights and benchmark. Numbers you may see quoted online are guesses.

What you can control:

- **Aim to be above the middle of the group.** A percentile above 50 means you beat more than half the people who took that test. Strong candidates for competitive roles will want to be well above that, but the exact line is the employer's.
- **Aim high on practice.** As our study guidance, if you can get about 75% or more of questions right on timed, well-made practice for a given test, you have a margin for nerves and harder questions on the day. This is our guidance, not a TestGorilla figure.
- **Finish every question.** Because blanks score zero and guesses score something, an unanswered question is the easiest mark to lose.

## Will you see your score?

Only if the employer chooses to share it. TestGorilla says that at the end of the assessment you are invited to create a TestGorilla profile, and if the employer has shared your results, you can view them there. TestGorilla does not share the questions or correct answers with candidates or employers.
